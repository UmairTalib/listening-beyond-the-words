# Pilot diagnostics and power

This page fixes how three steps after the pilot's result are computed: a summary of what training logged about the gain term, a probe of Voxtral's frozen audio encoder, and the main study's power, simulated again with the pilot's numbers. The study design, which is not in this repository, plans all three but leaves open how they are computed, and the [pilot analysis](pilot_analysis.md) left them for later (section "Not part of this analysis"). This page fills those points. It changes no rule of the [pilot protocol](pilot_protocol.md) or of the pilot analysis; where they seem to differ, they hold. Its settings are in `configs/pilot/probe.yaml` and `configs/pilot/power.yaml`. The page and both files are committed before any number of the three steps exists, so the commit date shows that they were fixed in advance.

Some numbers were seen before these rules were written: the pilot's AUROCs and differences (protocol, section "Decision rule", result of 2026-11-27), the per-epoch measures that the first six training runs logged (B0's round 1 and CPG's round 1, fold 1), and the gain-alive share of every B1 and CPG run. The power code was checked only on the study design's assumptions, with no pilot number. No rule on this page reads what was seen. B0's rows in the summary, and the gaps between the measured and the running statistics, were chosen after the six runs' measures were seen. All three steps are descriptive. They change no rule, result or decision of the pilot: the outcome stays "Green: beats B0", and the decision stays Go. The main study treats their numbers as seen, as it treats every pilot result (protocol, section "What the main study inherits").

## Departures from the study design

The study design differs from these rules in three places. All three were decided on 2026-11-27, after the pilot's result; only the first responds to it.
- **The probe also reads `rev`.** The study design probes `x0` and `x2`. `rev` was added on 2026-11-27, after the pilot's AUROCs were seen, because every arm was near chance on `rev`. `rev` is the pilot's view without words and one of the main study's unseen masks, so its probe shows whether the frozen encoder still carries the voice and the label there. Its rows say "post hoc", and no rule reads them, the main study's encoder check included.
- **How the voice targets are measured.** The study design measures them with Parselmouth. Here pitch variability comes from opensmile 2.6.0 with eGeMAPSv02, by the rules of the voice checks of `x1`; speech rate and pause ratio come from the pinned word times; and loudness comes from the stored samples. This reuses tested code and an exactly pinned tool. Parselmouth would need four or five settings fixed without data: the pitch floor and ceiling, a silence threshold and a shortest pause. The costs: eGeMAPS divides pitch variability by the mean pitch, so part of it is gender, hence the R² within gender; the aligner's word times end a little early (protocol, section "Speech used", bullet "Cut"), so pauses read slightly long; and every gap between words counts as a pause, with no shortest pause.
- **What sets the run noise in the power simulation.** The study design simulates the power with the pilot's noise figures, among them the difference between the two rounds' Δ as the training noise. With two rounds, that difference is one value per view, which cannot pin the run-to-run part of the noise. Here the simulation scans that part over the whole range that the measured agreement allows, and the difference between the rounds only scores each point's fit (section "The main study's power", bullet "Which number sets what").

## Gain-term summary

- **What.** The measures that training logged (protocol, section "Training", bullet "Logged measures"), summarised over the 10 CPG runs. Beside them, beyond the study design: the 10 B1 runs, which compute the gain term with weight 0, and B0's 10 runs for the measures that B0 logs (CE(`x0`), the CE push and the statistics of the `x0` bag means). Not here: the study design's gap between the training and the validation gain, because the pilot keeps no inner validation (protocol, section "Training", bullet "Who trains, and seeds").
- **Records.** Each kept run's `run.json`, checked against its pin in `configs/pilot/evaluate.yaml` before it is read (pilot analysis, section "Runs and pins"). Only the per-epoch measures in the record are read. No scores file, no step log and no label is opened.
- **Per epoch (1–3).** The loss and its parts: the CE of each view, the CE part and the hinge before λG. The active share over all bags, by class and by whether the words were right; the clamp share; and the share of steps with σ0 or σ1 at its floor. For `x0` and `x1`, the measured mean and spread of the bag means beside the average μ and σ, with the gap mean − μ and the ratio spread / σ (new here). |z0|/|z1|; the CE push, the gain push and its offset; and the skipped steps. B1's gain pushes are 0 by construction and are left out.
- **Summary.** Per arm, epoch and measure: the mean, the minimum and the maximum over the runs, and the number of runs. Per epoch also the pooled active share: the summed active bags over the summed bags, as for check F2 (pilot analysis, section "Checks first"). No rule reads the summary. A script on an OMNI login node writes it as `gain_terms.csv`.

## Pooled-encoder probe

- **What.** A probe is a simple model trained on a network's inner features, to test whether some information can be read from them. It changes nothing in Voxtral. This probe is the pilot's smaller version of the main study's encoder check: does Voxtral's frozen audio encoder pass on the voice? On `x0` the encoder hears the words and the voice, so `x2` (and `rev`, post hoc) speak to the voice. The probe is descriptive; with the outcome "Green: beats B0" it decides nothing. Not here: the study design's model on 119 voice features and its WavLM probe, which the pilot does not build.
- **Inputs.** The pinned caches of `x0`, `x2` and `rev` (`cache_runs` in `configs/pilot/data.yaml`), all 5,883 segments of each; the pinned `x0` clips and word times; the pinned labels with their PHQ-8 totals; and both rounds' partitions. The probes use round 1's five folds, so no participant is split between training and test. The draws follow the pilot analysis's rule, which reads the folds of both rounds (section "Intervals", bullet "Draws").
- **Pooling.** A cache file holds every vector of every 30 s chunk, the padding included: 1,500 vectors per chunk, one per 20 ms, each of 1,280 numbers (protocol, section "Encoder caches and zero-shot scores"). Vector f of chunk c covers the 20 ms that start at sample 480,000·c + 320·f. The probe keeps the vectors that start inside one of the segment's parts, which are the same in all three views, so the padding and the 300 ms gaps drop out. The clip over 30 s pools both its chunks. Pooling takes the mean and the population standard deviation of each number over the kept vectors, in float64: 2,560 features per segment.
- **Targets.** One value per segment, measured on `x0` and used for every view, because `x2` and `rev` keep the timing, the loudness and the pitch range of `x0`:
  - pitch variability: `F0semitoneFrom27.5Hz_sma3nz_stddevNorm`, by the rules of the voice checks (protocol, section "Clip versions", bullet "Voice measures"): on the copy at an RMS of 0.05, each part measured on its own, the parts weighted by voiced time;
  - speech rate: 40 words over the summed span of the parts, each part spanning from its first word's start to its last word's end;
  - pause ratio: the summed gaps between consecutive words within each part, over the same span;
  - loudness: 20·log10 of the RMS of the stored `x0` samples of the parts, in dBFS, the level that the encoder hears.

  The spans run between word times, so the parts' margins of up to 0.1 s stay out of both timing targets. Each part is measured on its own, so that one bad part cannot stop its clip. A part shorter than 60 ms, or one that opensmile cannot measure, is left out of pitch variability and of the 88 measures, and counted by kind. A segment without a value is left out of that target and counted. The targets are computed before any cache is read or any probe is fitted.
- **Probes.** A ridge regression from a segment's 2,560 features to each target, per view, scored by R². A logistic regression from a participant's features, the mean over their segments, to the label (PHQ-8 total of 10 or more), scored by AUROC. Both are linear models with a penalty on large weights. Gender is not a feature. The features are standardised on each fit's training rows.
- **Penalty.** Its strength is chosen inside each outer training set, by inner cross-validation over that set's four folds, each left out once. The grids are fixed, with one value per decade: ridge α from 1 to 10⁶, logistic C from 10⁻⁶ to 1. The value with the best mean inner R² (ridge) or the best mean inner fold AUROC (logistic) wins, and a tie goes to the stronger penalty. A choice at a grid end counts as one only if it beats its neighbour strictly; a tie there is reported as a plateau. An inner fit that does not converge skips its grid value and is counted; a final fit that does not converge stops the job. The test fold never shapes the penalty.
- **Controls.** The same folds, model, penalty rule and draws, on other inputs:
  - gender alone;
  - for every target, length alone: the number of kept vectors n, and 1/n. Speech rate is close to 2,000/n, and the encoder adds a position code to every vector, so pooled features can carry n;
  - for the label, also the 88 eGeMAPS measures of `x0`, as participant means; a missing mean gets the training fold's mean and is counted. This control is `x0`'s, not the study design's comparison on `x2`. It also differs from the voice probe on `x0` of the word check (0.4822; protocol, section "Clip versions"), which used 943 segments, C 0.1, centring by voice, and gender as a feature.
- **Numbers.** R² is the share of a target's variance that a probe explains in a test fold, against that fold's own mean: 1 is perfect, 0 is no better than the mean, and it can be negative. It is computed in each test fold, pooled and within gender, then averaged over the five folds. Within gender, each gender's mean target over the training rows is taken off the target, on the training and the test rows; this is the figure that counts for pitch variability. The label AUROC is computed per fold, with ties counted as one half, and then averaged (pilot analysis, section "The main numbers").
- **Intervals.** 80% and 95% percentile intervals (pilot analysis, section "Intervals") from 10,000 draws by the pilot analysis's rule, from seed 111, a seed no other step uses. The fitted probes stay fixed: a participant drawn k times counts k times, with all their segments. The same draws give each probe minus each of its controls, and `x2` − `x0` and `rev` − `x0`, so every difference is paired. The intervals cover the sampling of test participants, not the probes' fit or the choice of penalty.
- **Reading and limits**, printed with the table. Timing and loudness are expected to be predicted well, so the reading rests on pitch variability within gender and on the label probe. Pooled features give only a lower bound, because the language model reads the whole sequence; the planted-cue check that covers this is left for the thesis. Padding and gaps are left out. For scale, cited and not recomputed: probes on mean-pooled features of similar encoders reached 0.54–0.56 AUROC on DAIC-WOZ, against 0.75 for 119 voice features ([Perez-Toro et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06465)); and the arms' AUROCs in round 1, on the same folds (`x0`: zero-shot 0.7220, B0 0.6330, B1 0.6240, CPG 0.6791; `rev`: 0.5177, 0.5174, 0.5384, 0.5435; `reports/pilot/summary.csv`).
- **Output.** `probe.csv`, one row per view, outcome, model (encoder, gender only, length only, eGeMAPS 88, or a difference) and kind of R² (pooled or within gender), all with the same columns: the five fold values, their mean, both intervals, what was left out, and the chosen penalties with how often they fell at a grid end or on a plateau. A cell that cannot exist reads "not applicable".

## The main study's encoder check

The study design fixes when the reading rule of its encoder check is set, before the check runs, but not its values. The probe above computes the same numbers on the same people and frozen features, so the rule is fixed now, before the probe runs. It applies to the main study only; in the pilot the probe stays descriptive.
- Pitch variability counts as poorly predicted if its R² within gender on `x0` is below 0.10.
- A label probe on `x2` stays clearly below the model on 119 voice features on `x2` if the paired 95% interval of the probe minus that model lies wholly below 0.
- If either holds, the main study makes no claim about pitch and reads a null result without words, or with conflicting words, as a possible limit of the frozen encoder, as the study design says.
- The bound 0.10 has no source; it is set without data, as the study design asks. The second rule names a model that the pilot does not build.

## The main study's power

- **Power** is the chance that the main study's test says "CPG beats B0" when CPG is truly better by a given amount, the true gain. At a true gain of 0 it is the false-positive rate. The simulation reads no participant data: its inputs are two published tables, `reports/pilot/summary.csv` and `reports/pilot/fold_values.csv`, pinned by their SHA-256 in `configs/pilot/power.yaml`.
- **Model.** The study design's model, as this page reads it. A participant's score in an arm is the arm's class shift, set by its AUROC, plus a stable part, plus run noise that each fold-run draws anew. The stable parts of B0 and CPG correlate at r, and ρ is the correlation of an arm's scores between two runs. The paired arms share their bags, order and start (protocol, section "Training", bullet "Who trains, and seeds"); that shared part cancels in Δ and is not modelled. So ρ only splits the disagreement of B0 and CPG into a part that stays from run to run and a part that does not, and it is never quoted as an arm's repeatability. There are no segments or bags. On the study design's assumptions, the model reproduces its figures (bullet "Checks first").
- **Which number sets what.**
  - B0's AUROC, 0.6573 on `x0` and 0.5411 on `rev`, replaces the study design's assumptions (0.65 on `x0`, 0.52–0.58 without words).
  - The agreement of B0's and CPG's participant scores (their Spearman correlation within each fold, averaged), 0.9336 on `x0` and 0.8415 on `rev`, sets r at each ρ. The model's mean agreement in the pilot's design, at the pilot's B0 AUROC and Δ, must meet the measured one within 0.001, or the job stops. On `x0` the agreement lies above the range that the study design assumed (0.55–0.9). It reflects the pilot's pairing, so the main study is assumed to pair its arms the same way.
  - ρ takes 5 equal steps, from the smallest value that the agreement allows (r = 1: all disagreement is run noise) to 1 (none is).
  - The difference between the two rounds' Δ (−0.0028 on `x0`, 0.0350 on `rev`) sets nothing. For each ρ, the job reports the share of simulated pilots whose two rounds lie at least as far apart. With one value per view, this can flag only a gross misfit.
  - Δ's bootstrap standard error (0.0154 on `x0`, 0.0209 on `rev`) is printed beside the model's mean bootstrap standard error in the pilot's design, at each ρ.
  - Sensitivity rows, at the middle ρ: the agreement at the ends of its 80% interval (`x0` 0.9072–0.9386, `rev` 0.7978–0.8573); B0's AUROC at the ends of its 80% interval (`x0` 0.6050–0.7087, `rev` 0.4848–0.5985); and r set to match Δ's bootstrap standard error instead of the agreement. A sensitivity row that cannot meet its target within 0.001 is simulated at the r where the model comes closest and reported as not reached, with the model's value beside the target; the job goes on, because no decision reads these rows.
- **The main study**, as the study design plans it: the same 189 people, 3 new rounds of 5 folds stratified by label, with the pilot's class counts per fold. Δ is the mean of the 15 per-fold differences. The test is the pilot analysis's paired participant bootstrap (section "Intervals"), with 2,000 resamples per simulated study; the real test uses 10,000. The test says "CPG beats B0" if the α-quantile of the resampled Δ (linear) lies above 0.
- **α.** One-sided 0.05/3, 0.025 and 0.05; every number is given at all three. The main study tests three hypotheses: that CPG does better than B0 without words (on three unseen masks), that it follows the voice more than B0 when the words and the voice conflict, and that it does better on normal audio. After "Green: beats B0", the study design makes the test on normal audio primary beside the other two, with Holm's correction over the three: the smallest p-value is tested at α/3, the next at α/2 and the last at α. The test on normal audio therefore meets one of these three levels, by its rank.
- **The pilot inside each study.** Each simulated study also runs the pilot, on the same people at the same true gain. It calls the pilot Green if B0's mean AUROC on `x0` is at least 0.60 and Δ on `x0` is at least +0.03 (protocol, sections "Feasibility checks" and "Decision rule"). On `x0` the job reports the pass rate among the studies with a Green pilot, beside the rate in all studies. At a true gain of 0, this is the false-positive rate that the main study's test carries after this pilot: the test is primary only because the pilot was Green, and the two estimates share their people. `rev` is not conditioned, because the views are simulated apart.
- **Checks first**, set before any run, on the study design's assumptions only:
  1. At B0's AUROC of 0.65, in the study design's three scenarios, the model must reproduce the standard deviation of the pilot's Δ (0.026, 0.034 and 0.041) and, in the middle scenario, one arm's one-round AUROC standard deviation (0.044), each within 0.003. A miss stops the job as a code fault. The study design's figures for the main study are printed beside, not checked, because it does not name their test.
  2. On 400 studies each of the middle scenario, at true gains of 0 and 0.06, the decisions at 2,000 and at 10,000 resamples may differ in at most 1% of the studies at each α. Otherwise every table uses 10,000.
- **Grid.** True gains from 0.00 to 0.10 in steps of 0.01, on `x0` (the test on normal audio) and on `rev` (a stand-in for one of the unseen masks); 8,000 simulated studies per point, with the same draws for every gain. The Monte Carlo standard error, the error that comes only from simulating a finite number of studies, is then at most 0.006; among the Green pilots it depends on how many pilots are Green, and `power.csv` gives each of those rates with its own. The seed is 110 (101–109 are taken, and the probe uses 111), in fixed blocks, each with its own stream.
- **Outputs.** `power.csv`: per view, scenario (r, ρ and B0's AUROC), true gain and α, the power over new samples of people and, on `x0`, the power after a Green pilot with the number of Green pilots, each with its Monte Carlo standard error. At a true gain of 0 a rate can exceed α, because the participant bootstrap does not see training noise. `power_fit.csv`: per view and scenario, r; the model's agreement and mean bootstrap standard error beside the measured ones; the share of the round difference; the simulated main study's standard error (the standard deviation of its Δ at a true gain of 0) beside the pilot's measured one; the correlation of the pilot's and the main study's Δ; the true gain needed for 80% power at each α (linear between grid points, or "above 0.10"); and the arms' per-round AUROCs beside.
- **The power line.** It quotes the range over all five ρ points at each α, with the sensitivity rows beside. No ρ is chosen or dropped by its fit, which is only reported.
- **Limits**, printed with the tables: normal scores, without segments or bags; the same ρ in both arms, and no training part shared by the paired arms (it cancels in Δ); `rev` stands for one mask, not the mean over the main study's three; the study design's main study trains on about 128 people per fold, the pilot on about 151; and the power at a true gain equal to the pilot's Δ is no forecast, because the rule selected that Δ.

## Order

1. This page and its two settings files are committed before any number of the three steps exists. The review of the code may still change their wording before the jobs run; the last such commit dates the rules.
2. The code is tested on synthetic data only. The power code's tests use synthetic tables and the study design's assumptions; nobody runs it on the published tables before the release.
3. The three steps run once, from the release that holds their code (`v0.1.2`), and each refuses any other checkout: the summary on an OMNI login node, the probe and the simulation as two CPU jobs. There is no trial, because no decision reads these numbers.
4. No rule on this page changes once a number exists. A code fault, meaning code that does not do what this page says, as a synthetic test shows, is fixed with a test, released as the next `v0.1.x` and run again into a new folder; the rerun's numbers replace the first ones, and the result line names the fix. A job that fails without a code fault (a time limit, a lost node, memory) keeps its folder, marked failed, and runs once more from the same release into a new folder; nothing from a failed folder is read or published. A flaw in a rule itself is reported as a limitation, and any other reading is labelled post hoc.

## Data protection

- The probe runs only on OMNI, on a CPU. It reads the clips, word times, caches, labels and partitions of the 189 pilot participants only. Before anything else, it refuses labels that hold a session outside them or a label that disagrees with its PHQ-8 total. It writes no file per segment or participant: features, targets and predictions stay in memory.
- The summary reads only the training runs' records. The simulation reads only the two published tables, never a workspace file.
- All four tables go through the table writer that refuses identifiers, and each job's tests check that its log and its record hold aggregates only. Published: `gain_terms.csv`, `probe.csv`, `power.csv` and `power_fit.csv` in `reports/pilot/`, and a dated result line on this page.
