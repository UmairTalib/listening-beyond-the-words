# Pilot protocol

This page fixes how the pilot runs and how its result is read. It is committed here before the first training run; the commit date shows it was fixed in advance. Nothing on this page changes after test scores are seen. The only allowed changes are listed below and recorded in the settings change log.

## Design

| Item | Pilot |
| --- | --- |
| Question | Does CPG beat B0 on normal audio, and without words? |
| Data | E-DAIC sessions 300–492, 189 participants. Sessions 600–718 stay unopened. |
| Arms | Zero-shot Voxtral (scoring only), B0, B1 and CPG |
| Design | 2 rounds × 5 folds, split by participant, stratified by label and gender. 30 training runs. |
| Test views | `x0` (original audio) and `rev` (each 250 ms window played backwards, so the words are removed) |
| Main numbers | Δ = CPG − B0 in mean per-fold AUROC on each test view, over 10 fold-runs, with 80% and 95% intervals |
| Also reported | B1 − B0 and CPG − B1, values per round, zero-shot AUROC, Spearman correlation with the PHQ-8 totals |
| Compute | About 100 V100 GPU-hours; the pilot runs once, on speech with the interviewer cut out (section "Interviewer detector") |

## Speech used

Fixed on 12 November 2026, before any training. The settings are in `configs/pilot/data.yaml`.

- **Transcripts.** The pilot takes its words from the E-DAIC transcripts, because the DAIC-WOZ human transcripts had not arrived when the segments were built. They come from automatic speech recognition, hold the participant's lines and contain almost no fillers such as "um" and "uh". The words-only view `x1` therefore has no fillers, while `x0` keeps them. This is reported as a limitation. The DAIC-WOZ transcripts, which arrived on 2026-11-18, are used only for the times of the interviewer's and the participant's turns, never for their words (section "Interviewer detector").
- **Labels.** A participant is positive if the PHQ-8 total in the official split files is 10 or more. For every pilot participant this total equals the sum of the eight item scores. 57 of the 189 participants are positive. The binary column disagrees for 15 participants and is not used. A third label file gives a different total for 2 participants, which changes no label. Gender comes from the split files (87 female, 102 male); `detailed_lables.csv` codes it differently for 3 participants.
- **Interviewer filter.** The interviewer is mostly inaudible in these recordings, but leaks into some participant lines, and an assistant can be heard at the start of a few sessions. A transcript line is dropped if at least half of its speech, cut into 1.5 s windows, sounds more like the interviewer than like the participant to a speaker-verification model (WavLM x-vectors, pinned in `configs/pilot/models.yaml`), or if it contains one of 70 lines of her script in her own wording (10 until 2026-11-12, when 1.6% of the segments were found to still hold her questions). A line that lasts at least 1.5 s but holds no 0.75 s of continuously detected speech is dropped as well, because neither the model nor a listener has judged it; such lines hold 1.9% of the words and are balanced between the PHQ-8 classes. In a blind listening check of 35 lines, another voice was heard in an estimated 3.6% of all lines before the filter and in about 1.3% after it. The rest is reported as a limitation.
- **Word times.** A line of 30 s or more with fewer than one word per second has broken timestamps (a few words spread over minutes) and is dropped. The words of every kept line are then placed on the audio by forced alignment with wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self (pinned in `configs/pilot/models.yaml`); digits are spelled out for the aligner only. A line that cannot be aligned is dropped and counted.
- **Cut.** The original clip `x0` of a segment is cut from the session recording at its word times. Each part of the segment (its words from one line) keeps up to 0.1 s of audio before its first word and after its last word, because the aligner's word times end a little early. The margin never reaches more than halfway to the nearest other kept participant word, which is often less than 0.1 s away in the same line, and never past the recording. On all 6,207 segments, no margin passes the halfway point to a dropped line. With the aligner's padding, a part can still reach up to 0.3 s past the times of its transcript line, into audio that the interviewer filter did not judge (35 minutes over all segments); the listening check hears it. Each part fades in and out over 10 ms, so that the joins do not click, and the parts are joined by exactly 300 ms of silence. With full margins, the last word of one part and the first word of the next are therefore 0.5 s apart. The margin and the fade are set in `configs/pilot/data.yaml`.
- **Checks before training.** The share of dropped words must not differ between PHQ-8-positive and PHQ-8-negative participants (two-sided Mann–Whitney test, p ≥ 0.05); otherwise the filter is reviewed before any training. Twenty segments, drawn at random from all segments (seed 101), are cut as original clips and listened to before training, in random order and without their labels. Each clip is scaled to a common loudness and played over headphones at a fixed, clearly audible volume. Another voice means any sound made by anyone but the participant, speech or not: the interviewer, an assistant, background speech, a laugh or a "mhm", however faint. A clip that is still unclear after a second listen counts as another voice, and the listener notes whether it is heard at the start or end of a part or inside the speech. If another voice is heard in more than two of the 20 clips, the interviewer filter and the cut are reviewed. Result on 2026-11-12: another voice in 4 of the 20 clips (once at the edge of a part, three times inside the speech), so the filter and the cut are under review before any training.

### Interviewer detector

Pre-registered on 2026-11-13, before any run. Its settings are in `configs/pilot/data.yaml` (`ellie_detector`).

The speaker-verification filter misses the interviewer when she is short, faint or speaks in a pause of the participant. A test that scored each short stretch of speech on its own found her in only 2 of the 4 clips where she was heard and flagged 4 of the 16 clean clips, so it failed the rule set before its run. A dedicated detector is therefore added. It rests on one fact: the interviewer's lines are fixed recordings played in every session, so a sound that recurs almost identically in other sessions is her, while a participant's speech never recurs. Short sound events in the gaps between lines and in the pauses inside lines are matched across sessions, and those that recur in at least three sessions become templates of her clips. Every session is then scanned with the templates. A hit needs a match score above a threshold that is set only from decoys (the templates shifted up and down by 3 semitones) and a level that fits the interviewer's loudness in that session. A hit marks the template's full length as a zone. The detector uses no feature of the participant's voice.

Zones are applied to the speech in three ways. A line is dropped if zones cover at least half of it. A word that comes within 50 ms of a zone is dropped. A line is split where a zone lies between two kept words, so that the two pieces are joined by the 300 ms silence like two turns; the margins of the cut stop 20 ms before a zone. A rule for an assistant's voice before the interview (lines that end before the first zone of at least 1.5 s within the first two minutes are dropped) is used only if it drops at most 0.5% of the kept words.

The gates run in this order, and a failed gate stops the chain.

| Gate | Passes if |
| --- | --- |
| P1 Recurrence | The same interviewer phrase in two sessions is told apart from the same common word said by two participants with an AUROC of at least 0.95, and at least half of the same-phrase pairs score above the 99.9th percentile of the word pairs |
| P2 Library | At least 80 templates, after at most one rerun with a lower event threshold |
| D1 Known lines | At least 80% of the 266 lines that consist mostly of interviewer phrases are at least half covered by zones |
| D2 Decoys | Some threshold in the grid keeps the decoys' cost to at most 0.5% of the kept words on average and 0.75% for each decoy family; the smallest such threshold is used |
| D3 Planted copies | Copies of her clips from other sessions, planted at her level, are found in at least 90% of pauses, 90% of turn edges and 50% of partial overlaps |
| D4 Cost | At most 1% of common participant words touch a zone, and the zones remove at most 3% of the kept words |
| V4 Precision | In a blind set of 20 detections mixed with 20 controls, 2 s each, another voice is heard in at least 12 detections and at most 2 controls |
| V5 First clips | Zones meet at least 3 of the 4 clips where the interviewer was heard and at most 3 of the 16 clean clips |
| V6 Balance | After the rebuild, the dropped share of words does not differ between PHQ-8 classes (two-sided Mann–Whitney test, p ≥ 0.05) |
| V7 New clips | In 20 new segments (seed 102, none overlapping the first 20 clips), another voice in at most 2 |

Exactly one fallback follows a failure. If P1 or P2 fails, templates are built within each session instead of across sessions. If a later gate fails with the misses mostly over words or quiet, a small learned frame detector on frozen WavLM states is added on top of the zones, trained on the zones and on her clips planted over words; its threshold is the largest score that finds at least 70% of planted copies over words while dropping at most 1% more of the kept words. The fallback repeats the gates once, and V7 then uses seed 104. If V6 fails while the decoys' cost is balanced between the classes, the difference is real interviewer speech, and the researcher decides the case before training. If the fallback fails too, the researcher decides whether the pilot waits for the DAIC-WOZ transcripts with the interviewer's own times or runs with the leak reported as a limitation. No setting is ever tuned on a listened clip, and no rule is loosened.

Result on 2026-11-13, gate P: P1 failed. The same interviewer phrase in two sessions scored far above unrelated pairs (median 0.22 against 0.03 for different phrases), but not reliably above the same word said by two participants: AUROC 0.929 for the fine-structure variant (0.857 for the whitened spectrum), and only 23% of same-phrase pairs above the null's 99.9th percentile (40%). P2 passed with 587 templates, but the chain stops at P1.

Decision on 2026-11-13, before any training. The fallback is not run: none of the 266 known interviewer lines has its phrase transcribed three or more times in its own session, so a library built within sessions could not meet D1. The pilot therefore runs twice, with the same settings.

- **First run, preliminary.** It uses the speech rebuilt with the 70 scripted lines dropped. The interviewer can still be heard in some segments (in 4 of the 20 listened clips); this is reported as a limitation.
- **Second run, the main result.** When the DAIC-WOZ transcripts with the interviewer's own times arrive, every stretch where she speaks is cut out and the speech is rebuilt. A new blind listening check of 20 segments (seed 102, none overlapping the first 20 clips) must find another voice in at most 2. Then all runs are repeated.
- **Fixed now.** The decision rule is read on the second run; the first run only measures how much the leak changed the result. Settings fixed during the first run (the margin m and the number of epochs) carry over unchanged, with no new tuning.
- **Deadline.** If the transcripts have not arrived by 2026-11-27, an interviewer finder that re-transcribes the audio with the pinned Whisper model is tried first, with its own rules fixed before it runs.

Decision on 2026-11-18, before any training. The DAIC-WOZ human transcripts arrived, so the pilot runs once, on the speech with the interviewer cut out; this replaces the two runs above. The preliminary run on the current speech and the Whisper finder are dropped.

- **Source.** The transcripts come from the official DAIC-WOZ release of USC ICT. Each is checked against the CRC-32 in its archive, and every recording in the release is byte-identical to the E-DAIC recording of the same session, so the transcript times fit the E-DAIC audio exactly as they fit the DAIC-WOZ audio; four sessions whose times are offset from their recording are shifted by the cut rule below. The official archive of session 440 is cut off on the USC server, so its transcript comes from a public copy whose other 188 transcripts are byte-identical to the official files; seven further copies agree with it.
- **Cut.** Every stretch where the interviewer speaks is cut out by her times in these transcripts. Sessions 451, 458 and 480 have no interviewer turns in the release; for them only the words inside the participant's own turns are kept. The exact rule, including how known timing offsets and interruptions are handled, is fixed on this page before the speech is rebuilt.
- **Check.** A new blind listening check of 20 segments (seed 102, none overlapping the first 20 clips) must find another voice in at most 2 before any training.

Cut rule, fixed on 2026-11-18 before the speech is rebuilt. Its settings are in `configs/pilot/data.yaml` (`daicwoz_cut`). The interviewer filter of the section "Speech used" stays as it is: the words of the lines it keeps are aligned again, and then this rule decides which of them remain. A turn is one row of a DAIC-WOZ transcript with valid times; rows that are blank or hold only a sync marker are not turns.

- **Participant's turns.** A word is kept only if its middle lies inside one of the participant's turns and it does not come within 200 ms of one of the interviewer's turns. A word from s to e comes within 200 ms of a turn from a to b if s < b + 200 ms and e > a − 200 ms. Keeping only the participant's turns, instead of only removing the interviewer's, also drops words in stretches that no participant turn covers. The guard is wider than the interviewer detector's 50 ms, because the detector's zones already reached 150 ms past the sound they matched, while the transcript's turns have no such padding. Sessions 451, 458 and 480 have no interviewer turns, so only the participant's turns decide for them.
- **Interview only.** A word that begins before the interviewer's first turn starts or ends after her last turn ends is dropped. Before her first turn the participant may talk with the research staff, and an assistant can be heard at the start of a few sessions; after her last turn the interview is over. On the word times of 2026-11-12, this drops 1 word before her first turn and 132 words after her last turn (in 70 sessions) that the other conditions keep. The rule does not apply to sessions 451, 458 and 480.
- **Offsets.** In sessions 318, 321, 341 and 362 the transcript times are offset from the recording. Every time in these four transcripts is shifted by the offset that the timing check found (`scripts/check_daicwoz_timing.py`: the shift, in 0.1 s steps from −90 s to +90 s, that puts the most word middles inside the participant's turns): +34.1 s, +3.5 s, +5.9 s and +16.7 s. With these, 95.7–99.1% of the aligned words fall inside the participant's turns, close to the median of 97.5% over all sessions. The public preprocessing code [daic_woz_process](https://github.com/adbailey1/daic_woz_process/blob/8b5f8ff5c510df9904e0750f1a806c29e649c5cf/config_files/config_process.py), which does not say how its offsets were found, adds +34.32, +3.84, +6.19 and +16.86 s instead (82.0–93.3%). The shifts that put the fewest words inside the interviewer's turns (+34.48, +3.72, +6.24 and +15.70 s) are not used, because the real overlaps between the two speakers, which the transcripts mark, also move them. The public offsets lie within 0.34 s of ours, and those shifts within 0.38 s in three of the four sessions, so the guard in these four sessions is 400 ms. No other session has a best shift of more than 0.5 s in either direction, and no other session is shifted.
- **Interruptions.** The DAIC-WOZ documentation names two interruptions in which another person enters the room: session 373 from about 5:52 to 7:00 and session 444 from about 4:46 to 6:27. These times are approximate, so each interruption is widened by 10 s on each side and treated like an interviewer turn. The widened windows also contain the windows in the public code above (373: 6:35–7:08; 444: 4:46–6:27).
- **Parts and margins.** A part of a segment is a run of its kept words from one transcript line with no dropped word and no interviewer turn between them; parts are joined by the 300 ms silence as before. The audio between two kept words of one part stays in the clip, so the participant's pauses are kept. In sessions 451, 458 and 480, whose interviewer turns are missing, a part also ends where one participant turn ends and the next begins, so that no audio from a gap between two participant turns is used there. For the same reason, a margin there also stops at the edge of the participant's turn, or at the edge of a kept word that runs past it (added on 2026-11-18, before the listening check, because the first build kept 10.5 s of margin audio in these gaps). A margin stops 20 ms short of an interviewer turn on either side and never reaches more than halfway to a dropped word. Everything else in the cut stays as in the section "Speech used".
- **Cost.** On the word times of 2026-11-12, which were aligned while the interviewer filter still used 10 of her lines, the rule drops 4.77% of the aligned words, with no significant difference between the PHQ-8 classes (two-sided Mann–Whitney test, p = 0.75) or the genders (p = 0.26; 0.27 until 2026-11-25, section "Training"). Every participant keeps at least 129 words; one keeps fewer than 160 (4 segments, one bag) and 38 keep fewer than 640 (16 segments). How their segments are used is fixed in the row "Segments and bags" of the fixed settings, before training.
- **Checks.** On the rebuilt speech, the balance test of the section "Speech used" is repeated on the share of each participant's transcript words that are dropped in all (by the filter, the word times and this rule), and the number of parts per segment is reported for each PHQ-8 class. The new listening check (seed 102) replaces the first one, which is not repeated.
- **If a check fails.** If the new listening check hears another voice in more than 2 of the 20 clips, the guard is widened once to 500 ms in every session, the speech is rebuilt, and 20 new segments (seed 104, none overlapping the 40 clips heard before) are checked the same way. If that check fails too, the researcher decides before any training whether the pilot runs with the leak reported as a limitation. If the dropped share differs between the PHQ-8 classes (p < 0.05), the rule is kept and the difference is reported by cause: the interviewer's turns, stretches outside the participant's turns, the interview's limits and the interruptions. No setting of this rule is tuned on a listened clip, and no rule is loosened.

Result on 2026-11-18, on the speech rebuilt with this rule (250,016 words aligned from the selection with 70 of her lines): the rule drops 4.48% of the aligned words (by her turns 6,970 words, outside the participant's turns 3,920, at the interview's limits 133, by the interruptions 183). The share of each participant's transcript words dropped in all does not differ between the PHQ-8 classes (two-sided Mann–Whitney test, p = 0.50) or the genders (p = 0.98), so the balance check passes. A segment has a mean of 4.06 parts for negative and 4.24 for positive participants (means per participant, p = 0.28). All 189 participants keep segments, 5,883 in all (3 to 103 each); one has fewer than 4 and 39 have fewer than 16. One x0 clip is longer than 30 s (37.4 s). The new listening check (seed 102, 20 clips from 19 sessions, none sharing audio with the clips heard before) found another voice in none of the 20 clips, so it passes and the speech is used as built.

## Clip versions

Fixed on 2026-11-20, before any clip version is built. The settings are in `configs/pilot/data.yaml` (`views` and `word_check`). `x0` is cut as in the section "Speech used" and by the cut rule of 2026-11-18 (section "Interviewer detector"), and the other views are made from the same parts. The settings that only `x1` has, such as its voices and the text it reads, are added here and to `configs/pilot/data.yaml` before `x1` is built. The rules of the word check, including the checks of `x1`, are added before any clip is transcribed.

- **The part is the unit.** Every view is built part by part, and its parts are joined by exactly 300 ms of silence, as in `x0`. `x1` makes one Kokoro call per part, and `x2` filters each part of `x0` on its own. For `rev`, each part's audio, as cut for `x0` but before its fades, is played backwards in 250 ms windows that start at the part's first sample; a shorter last window is reversed too. The part then gets the same 10 ms fades as `x0`, so that it starts and ends at exactly 0. The backup of check F4 plays each whole part backwards, also before the fades. The study design, which is not in this repository, said "turn" for `x1`'s calls and for this backup, and so did check F4 below for the backup until 2026-11-20. A part is meant there, and a turn stays one row of a DAIC-WOZ transcript. With the part as the unit, every view has the same pieces and the same silent gaps, and no filter echo or reversed window reaches into a gap.
- **Re-voiced clip.** Fixed on 2026-11-22, before `x1` is built. Kokoro-82M (pinned in `configs/pilot/models.yaml`; kokoro 0.9.4, with misaki 0.9.4 as its text front end; US English, speed 1.0, evaluation mode, so no random dropout) reads each part's words in one of two voices of the participant's gender: af_heart or af_bella for women, am_michael or am_fenrir for men. Within each gender, participants are ordered by PHQ-8 class (negative first) and then by ID, and alternate between the two voices along that order, starting with af_heart and am_michael. Each voice thus gets half of the negative and half of the positive participants of its gender, give or take one, so that the voice is balanced against the label. The study design, which is not in this repository, assigned the voices by participant ID alone. The segments store each word in lower case, with apostrophes dropped and every other character that is not a letter or a digit turned into a space, so "I'm" is stored as "im" and "x-ray" as "x" and "ray". Kokoro reads the words as the E-DAIC transcript writes them instead: capitals and apostrophes are kept (a typographic apostrophe becomes a plain one), and every other mark becomes a space. A word as written is used only when all its stored words lie in the part; otherwise the part's stored words are used, for example when "x-ray" is split at a part edge. Turning this text back into stored words gives exactly the part's words. Then every word whose stored form is made only of digits is written out in words, by its value, as misaki reads those digits as a number standing on its own (num2words 0.5.14): four digits as a year and any other number as a whole number, without "and". So "1990" is read "nineteen ninety", "125" "one hundred twenty five" and "007" "seven"; Kokoro reads the digits in other words, such as "1990s" or "3rd", itself. After an apostrophe, as in "'05", misaki itself would read the digits one by one or in pairs ("zero five"); the rule reads them by their value ("five"), like any other number. This rule was added on 2026-11-23, after the dry run of the check job (job 12574475) had found one word without a sound, and before any clip was rendered. Read against misaki's code, the text-free classes that the job printed show that it was a word made only of digits that spaCy's tagger, which misaki uses, had tagged as punctuation, and misaki gives such a word no sound; the word itself was not looked at. A number written out in words cannot be silenced that way. Words next to a number may now be read differently, because spaCy may tag them differently or because misaki reads some words, such as "the" or "to", by the sound that follows them; this can change their stress or, rarely, their sounds, and the dry run counts them. Words that keep a sound but lose a piece to such tagging, such as the digits of "1990s", are counted and reported and never change this mapping. Words outside Kokoro's lexicon get their sounds from espeak-ng 1.52.0. Each part's call is seeded from seed 106, the segment and the part's position. Kokoro's 24 kHz output is resampled to 16 kHz with soxr. Each part is then trimmed at both ends where it is more than 40 dB below its peak, keeping 10 ms beyond the first and last louder sample, and gets the same 10 ms fades as `x0`, which fall in those 10 ms. The parts are joined by 300 ms of silence, and the clip's RMS, gaps included, is set to 0.05 before the peak guard (bullet "Levels"). Before any clip is rendered, a dry run of Kokoro's text front end on every part must give every word a sound; if it does not, only this text mapping may change, dated here. Then 20 segments outside the word-check sample (seed 106) are rendered, and the researcher listens to 10 of them. A listen can expose a fault in the code, which is fixed for all clips, but no setting of `x1` is tuned on a listened clip.
- **Muffled clip.** The filter of `x2` is an 8th-order Butterworth low-pass whose −3 dB point is at 400 Hz. It runs forwards and then backwards, so that nothing shifts in time; the two passes together are 6 dB down at 400 Hz. After the filter, each part gets the same 10 ms fades as `x0` again (added on 2026-11-22, before any clip version is built), because the filter spreads each part's edges by a few milliseconds: without them the muffled parts would no longer start and end at exactly 0, and each join with a silent gap would get a small step that can click. The clip's RMS is then set to that of `x0`.
- **Levels.** `x0` is never rescaled: it keeps the recording's level. `x1` is set to an RMS of 0.05 and `x2` to that of `x0`. If the peak of an `x1` or `x2` clip is then above 0.99, the whole clip is scaled down to a peak of 0.99, so that no sample reaches full scale. Such clips are counted, and they stay below their target RMS. `rev` only reorders the recording's own samples and fades each part like `x0`, so its peak never exceeds the recording's and it needs no such guard.
- **Files.** Every view is stored in the workspace as 16 kHz mono 16-bit FLAC, with a manifest that gives each file's length, RMS, peak and SHA-256 and the start and end sample of each part. Each built view is pinned in `configs/pilot/data.yaml` by the SHA-256 of its manifest.
- **Word-check sample.** The word check (check F4 and the checks of `x1`) transcribes five segments per participant, drawn at random with seed 105, or all of a participant's segments if there are fewer: at most 943 segments. The sample is drawn and pinned before any clip is transcribed.
- **Word check.** Fixed on 2026-11-24, before it is coded and before any clip is transcribed. The settings are in `configs/pilot/data.yaml` (`word_check`). Both ASR systems read all four clip versions of the 943 segments of the word-check sample. Each clip is first checked against its manifest: the SHA-256 of its file and of its samples, 16 kHz mono, its length and its silent gaps. The reference of every view is the segment's 40 stored words. For `x1` this is the text that Kokoro read, once both pass the text rule below, apart from words that mix digits and letters, such as "1990s", whose digits misaki read itself. Labels and gender come from the pinned labels, the folds from round 1 (seed 101) of the pinned partitions, and each participant's `x1` voice from the rule of the bullet "Re-voiced clip".
  - **ASR systems.** Whisper large-v3 runs in faster-whisper 1.2.1 and wav2vec2-large-960h-lv60-self in transformers, both at their pinned revisions and offline. Whisper runs in float16, in English, with a beam of 5 and at temperature 0 only, so that it never samples at random. It does not see the text of earlier windows and uses no voice-activity filter. A window is the 30 s of audio that Whisper reads at once; almost every clip is one window. Whisper's rules that drop a window's text or decode it again (by its compression ratio, its mean log-probability, its no-speech probability or a silence around a suspected hallucination) are all off, and every option that can change its text is written into `configs/pilot/data.yaml`. By default, a window that Whisper judges silent loses all its text, and an empty `rev` clip makes both rules of F4 easier to pass: on synthetic reversed speech, the defaults would have emptied 3 of 14 clips. The windows that the defaults would have dropped are counted and reported. wav2vec2 runs in float32, its own precision, one clip at a time, with greedy decoding: the most likely letter in each 20 ms frame. Its special symbols are dropped and counted. Both ASR systems read the same numbers: the stored 16-bit samples divided by 32,768. Before any participant clip is read, each ASR system reads the tool check's clips of a fixed public text in seven voices; a WER above 0.10 on them stops the job as a fault.
  - **Text.** One rule turns the references, the ASR systems' outputs and the text of the leakage classifiers into words. It lowers the case, drops apostrophes and turns every other mark into a space, as the segments store their words. It then splits each word into runs of letters and runs of digits, and writes each run of digits out as `x1` reads it: four digits as a year and any other number as a whole number, without "and" (digit by digit if num2words cannot read the number, as from 307 digits on). So "1990" becomes "nineteen ninety" and "125" becomes "one hundred twenty five". Two steps then repeat until nothing changes: two or more one-letter words in a row are joined into one word ("p t s d" becomes "ptsd", as the segments store it), and hesitations are dropped: words made only of h and m, with or without u in front (such as "um", "uhh", "hmm", "mm" and "mhm"), and "er", "erm" and "ah", each with any number of repeated letters. Joining comes first, so the "h" of "a d h d" stays, and the repeat makes sure that the rule changes nothing when it runs twice. Real one-letter words in a row, such as "a" and "i", are joined too, the same way on both sides. Last, five spellings are merged: "ok" becomes "okay", "alright" becomes "all right", and "gonna", "wanna" and "gotta" become "going to", "want to" and "got to". Numbers follow `x1` because its reference is then exactly the text that Kokoro read, and because most people say years that way. The aligner's spelling ("one thousand nine hundred ninety") would charge an ASR system that writes what it hears, as wav2vec2 does, up to four errors for a year that it heard correctly. The other steps remove differences of spelling, not of hearing: on the same synthetic speech, one ASR system wrote "O K" where the other wrote "Okay", and wav2vec2 wrote "P T S D" for PTSD.
  - **WER and words heard.** The WER of a view is counted for each ASR system over all its words at once, with jiwer 4.0.0: substitutions, deletions and insertions, divided by the words of the references. Inserted words count, so the WER can exceed 1. The hits and the three error counts are reported with it. An empty reference is refused before any clip is transcribed. The words heard (hits divided by the words of the references) and their chance level are reported for every view and ASR system, but neither is a rule. For the chance level, each output is also scored against another participant's words: participants are taken in ID order, each is paired with the participant 94 places further on (wrapping round), and its k-th sampled segment in ID order, counted from 0, with that participant's segment k mod n, where n is that participant's number of sampled segments.
  - **Check F4.** `rev` passes if each ASR system reaches a WER of at least 0.80 and a leakage AUROC of at most 0.60: four point estimates. The leakage AUROC comes from one text classifier per fold of round 1. Each is trained on the stored words of every segment of the fold's training participants, each segment with its participant's label: TF-IDF (word counts weighted by how rare each word is) of the words and word pairs that occur in at least 2 training segments, every word counted (one-letter words such as "i" too), and a logistic regression with C 1.0 (L2, at most 5,000 iterations). The five classifiers are trained before any clip is transcribed, and one that does not converge stops the job. A test participant's sampled segments are scored from each ASR system's output, after the text rule, by the classifier of the participant's own fold only; a text that is empty or holds no word the classifier knows gets the classifier's intercept as its score. A participant's score is the mean log-odds (the classifier's raw score, before it becomes a probability) over those segments. Each fold's AUROC counts ties as one half, and the leakage AUROC is the mean of the five fold AUROCs, never one AUROC over all participants. The same five classifiers serve every view and ASR system. If the `x0` control calls for the rerun (next bullet) while both of `rev`'s WERs are at least 0.80, F4 waits for it: the sample's leakage is reported as provisional, and F4 is decided on the rerun's. A WER below 0.80 fails F4 at once, because the rerun keeps the sample's WER. `x2` gets the same WER, words heard and leakage, reported only.
  - **The `x0` control.** For each ASR system, `x0` must reach a WER of at most 0.40 and a leakage AUROC whose 95% interval lies above 0.5. This shows that the ASR systems and the classifiers find the words and the label where both are present. The interval is the bootstrap of the main numbers (fixed settings, row "Intervals"): participants are drawn with replacement within each PHQ-8 class over all 189, while the classifiers and the participants' scores stay fixed; each fold's AUROC is computed again on the drawn participants, and the five are averaged; a resample in which a fold has only one class is drawn again and counted; the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of 10,000 resamples bound the interval. Its random numbers come from a stream of their own, derived from seed 105 and the bootstrap's name, not from the stream that drew the sample. The draws depend only on the labels and the folds, so they are made once, before any clip is transcribed, and shared by every view and ASR system. If an interval includes 0.5 (its lower end at most 0.5 and its upper end at least 0.5), the leakage part runs once more, on all segments of every participant, for `x0` and `rev` with both ASR systems, as its own GPU job. Its leakage AUROCs and `x0`'s intervals replace the sample's, whichever way they move, and F4's leakage is read after it. The WER stays that of the sample, so the bound of 0.40 is read on the sample, and the rerun's WER is reported only. A WER above 0.40 alone never calls for the rerun, and an interval wholly below 0.5 fails the control without one. A control that still fails is reported as a weakness of the check and never changes F4.
  - **Words of `x1`.** Each ASR system must reach a WER of at most 0.15 on `x1`. Reported beside it: the WER of the segments that have a part of one or two words, against the rest, and the errors of `x1` by word class, with those of `x0` as the baseline. The classes hold no text: a number written out in words (four digits or other), a word that mixes digits and letters, a one-letter word, the first or last word of a part, a word in a part of one or two words, and, from the transcript, a word in capitals, a word with an apostrophe, a word that Kokoro read as its stored words and a word split at a part edge. Substitutions and deletions count for the class of their reference word, and an inserted word counts for the words on either side.
  - **Voice measures.** The voice checks of `x1` use opensmile 2.6.0 with eGeMAPSv02 (88 measures). Three of them make the ICC gate: pitch variability (`F0semitoneFrom27.5Hz_sma3nz_stddevNorm`), loudness variability (`loudness_sma3_stddevNorm`) and voiced stretches per second (`VoicedSegmentsPerSec`). Each part is measured on its own, without the gaps, by its view's own part bounds. The measures are taken on a copy of each `x0` and `x1` clip, made in memory at `x1`'s level: an RMS of 0.05 over the clip, gaps included, then the 0.99 peak guard. `x1` is at that level already, and no stored file changes. opensmile counts a frame as unvoiced below a fixed level, and `x0` is about 15 dB quieter than `x1` (median RMS 0.0087), so at its stored level soft voiced sounds could lose their pitch in `x0` only. The cost is that `x0`'s probe no longer sees each recording's level, which may carry part of the voice signal or only the recording channel. `x0`'s voiced time at its stored level is reported as a share of that on the copy. A segment's value is the mean over its parts, weighted by what the measure is taken on: measures of voiced frames by voiced time, measures of unvoiced frames by unvoiced time, the sound level as a power mean, and all others by part length. Of the six timing measures, loudness peaks per second go by part length, the mean of the voiced stretches' lengths by the number of stretches and their spread pooled over all voiced stretches of the segment, and the mean and spread of the unvoiced stretches' lengths by unvoiced time. The first voiced stretch of each part is not counted, because every part starts one in both views, so the number of parts would otherwise count as agreement: voiced stretches per second is the sum over the parts of n − 1 (at least 0) divided by their summed length, where n is a part's number of voiced stretches as opensmile counts them. A part shorter than 60 ms gets no values, and a part without a voiced frame gets none for the measures of voiced frames (opensmile writes 0 there). Parts without a value are left out and counted; a segment without any part value has no value for that measure.
  - **ICC gate.** ICC(C,1), the Shrout–Fleiss ICC(3,1) of the glossary, is computed over the sampled segments that have a value in both views. `x1` fails the gate if, within either gender, any of the three measures has an ICC above 0.30: six values. Before the ICC, the gap between each gender's two voices is removed: for each gender and measure, the mean of each `x1` voice is subtracted from both views, with `x0` grouped by the participant's `x1` voice and the means taken over the same paired segments. A gap between the two voices adds variance to `x1` that `x0` does not share, and that lowers the ICC: on synthetic data, a true ICC of 0.40 passed the gate without centring in 50% of draws when the two voices lay two within-voice standard deviations apart, and in 0.1% with it. A negative ICC is kept as it is and passes. A value that cannot be computed, because neither view varies, stops the job as a fault and is never compared with 0.30. Reported beside the gate: the ICC without centring within each gender, the pooled ICC, the ICC of the whole clips (within each gender and pooled), Pearson's r beside each, and each voice's offset per measure.
  - **Voice probe.** The probe predicts the label from all 88 measures, averaged per participant over the sample, in the folds of round 1. In each fold, the mean of each `x1` voice over the training participants is subtracted from `x1`'s measures and, with the same voice groups, from `x0`'s. Gender is then added as one more feature, not centred, and the features are scaled on the training participants. The model is a logistic regression with C 0.1 (L2, at most 5,000 iterations), and the result is the mean of the five fold AUROCs. `x0` and `x1` go through exactly the same steps, so their probes differ only in the audio. The control is gender alone, built the same way. Its AUROC follows from the counts of each fold (0.5599 with these folds, which puts the bar at 0.6099), and the job computes it from the folds all the same. The probe counts only if `x0`'s probe is more than 0.05 above the control; `x1` then fails if its own probe is more than 0.05 above the control. If the probe does not count, the ICCs decide alone, and the output says so. A participant without a value gets its voice's training mean, which is 0 after centring, and is counted. Over all participants, each voice holds half of each PHQ-8 class of its gender, so a training fold's link between voice and label is the mirror image of its test fold's; without the centring this would push `x1`'s probe about 0.0075 AUROC towards passing. Each voice has one gender, so the centring also removes the gender difference, which adding gender back restores: the probe keeps what its control uses. C 0.1 restrains the model more than C 1.0, which barely does with 88 measures and about 151 training participants per fold. On synthetic data with a voice signal of AUROC 0.70, `x0`'s probe beat the bar in 60% of draws at C 0.1 against 37% at C 1.0, while a probe without signal beyond gender beat it in 5.5% against 4.5%.
  - **Departures from the study design.** The study design, which is not in this repository, differs from these rules in five places. It reads one ICC over all participants; here the gate is read within each gender, because pitch variability is divided by the mean pitch, which differs between women and men, and `x1` keeps the gender on purpose, so a pooled ICC would count gender as voice; the pooled ICC is reported. It asks only that the probe on `x1` do no better than the gender-only control while the probe on `x0` does better, without a margin; here both use the margin of 0.05, about one standard deviation of a mean of five fold AUROCs at this size, because on synthetic data a probe without signal beyond gender beat the control in about 20% of draws without a margin, and in about 6% with it. It asks for a low WER on `x0` without a number; here the bound is 0.40, half of `rev`'s. It asks that any failure of `x1` be put right before CPG is first trained; here only a failed word rule is fixed (next bullet), and a failed voice rule is reported as a limitation: Kokoro reads only the text and a voice name, so a voice failure can only come from what `x0` and `x1` share by design (the words, the parts and the gender), which no setting of `x1` can remove. Its probe takes the measures as they are; here they are centred within each `x1` voice, with gender added, so that the probe and its gender-only control differ only in the audio.
  - **If a check fails.** If `rev` fails F4, the backup (each whole part played backwards, before `x0`'s fades) is built and checked once in the same way: on the same sample, with both ASR systems and the same classifiers, and with its leakage read on all segments if the rerun ran. If the backup fails too, the researcher decides before training. If `x1` fails its word rule, the only allowed fix is a dated change of its text mapping, chosen from the error counts by word class, so that the fix stays a general rule. `x1` is then rendered again once, through its check job (dry run, test render and listening), and checked again once on a new sample: five segments per participant, or all if there are fewer, drawn with seed 107 from all segments. A segment may lie in both samples; such segments are counted. The re-check repeats the rules of `x1` (words, ICC and probe) and replaces their first results, while F4 and the `x0` control stand. Any other cause, or a second failure, goes to the researcher before any B1 or CPG run. If `x1` fails its voice rule, it is kept as built, the failure is reported as a limitation, and B1 and CPG go ahead. Until the checks of `x1` are finished, including a re-check if one runs, no test-fold score is computed, zero-shot or trained; caches and code may go on.
  - **Reading the rules.** Every rule is read on its point estimate; only the `x0` control uses an interval. "At least" and "at most" include the bound, while "more than" and "above" do not. Values are compared unrounded and printed with 4 decimals. F4 reads PASS, FAIL or WAITS FOR THE RERUN, the `x0` control PASS, FAIL or RERUN NEEDED, and the words and the voice of `x1` each PASS or FAIL. The voice of `x1` fails if any of the six gate ICCs is above 0.30, or if the probe counts and `x1`'s probe is more than 0.05 above the control. No rule is loosened or tuned after results that use the labels, and a failed control or check of `x1` never loosens F4. The ASR systems' text and every number per segment or participant stay in the workspace on OMNI. Only aggregates are printed or committed, and the classifiers' words and weights are never printed or saved.

## Encoder caches and zero-shot scores

Fixed on 2026-11-25, before any cache is built and before any zero-shot score is computed. The settings are in `configs/pilot/data.yaml` (`encoder_cache`) and `configs/pilot/zeroshot.yaml`.

- **What is cached.** Voxtral's audio encoder is frozen, so it runs once on every segment of every clip version (`x0`, `x1`, `x2` and `rev`), and training and scoring read its stored output instead. The stored output is the encoder's own, taken before the adapter: every vector of every 30 s chunk, the padding included, because the language model reads them all, in fp16, the format the encoder computes in. There is one file per segment and version, listed with its SHA-256, its clip's SHA-256 and its length, and each version's cache is pinned like the clip versions.
- **The clip over 30 s.** One segment's `x0` clip is 37.4 s long, and so are its `x2` and `rev` clips. They are read as Voxtral reads any long clip: in two 30 s chunks, so that the language model sees 750 audio vectors instead of 375. No segment is left out.
- **Cache equals audio.** A score computed from the cache must equal the score computed from the audio bit for bit; there is no tolerance. To make this possible, every job of this step runs the encoder on one clip at a time, with the same numeric settings, which it records, and on one kind of GPU node if the nodes differ in their CPU model, because the encoder's input features are computed on the CPU. On synthetic clips, the tests compare scores. On real clips, a check job computes the log-mel features and the encoder output of 25 segments per version (drawn with seed 108), and of every two-chunk segment, again from the audio, and checks by their SHA-256 that they are byte for byte the same as at build time, the features first, so that a difference on the CPU side is told apart from one on the GPU side. It compares no scores, because every participant lies in a test fold. The zero-shot job first scores 20 segments of each scored version (`x0`, `x1` and `rev`, drawn with seed 108), and every two-chunk segment, from the audio and from the cache, and stops unless both agree bit for bit. A difference is a fault, not noise: it is found and fixed in the code or by the choice of nodes, and the check runs again under the same rule, which is never loosened.
- **Overflow.** The model computes in fp16, which holds no value above 65,504. Every layer of the encoder and of the language model is watched: its output and its attention and feed-forward parts. So are the encoder's input convolutions, the adapter between the two, both final norms, and the logits whenever scores are computed. A value at the encoder's limit (64,512 or more in size) or a value that is not finite flags the layer. Before any score exists, the check job also runs the adapter and the language model without its output layer on the 25 longest clips of each version, so that the watch covers them too. The fallback is fixed now: a flagged layer runs in fp32, and is timed; if the overflow lies in the adapter's output or in the sum carried from layer to layer, that sum stays in fp32 from there on. A flag in the encoder fails its cache run: that cache is never pinned and is built again with the fallback. A flag in the adapter or the language model leaves the caches standing; the zero-shot job and training then use the fallback, and the zero-shot job refuses to start until it is in place. A zero-shot run with a flag, or with a score that is not finite, writes no scores and runs again with the fallback. If the fallback cannot run, or makes check F5 fail, the researcher decides before any training run. This fallback is pre-set, like those of the feasibility checks (section "Allowed setting changes"): when it is used, it is dated in the settings change log, with the watch's counts as its evidence.
- **Zero-shot scores.** The zero-shot arm scores every segment of `x0` and `rev`, the two test conditions, and of `x1`, whose scores start the running statistics (next bullet), with the prompt and score of the trained arms. It runs only after the rules of the training code are fixed and dated here, so that no setting is written after any test score exists. It reads no labels and prints only numbers that need none (counts, whether all are finite, means, spreads, percentiles and the share above 0); its AUROC is computed later by the analysis code. Every test-fold score of the pilot, zero-shot or trained, comes from the same scoring code, one clip at a time, from the cache.
- **Start of the running statistics.** Each CPG run starts the mean and spread (the population SD) of the bag means of `x0` and `x1` from its own first 32 bags: the first 8 steps of its training sampler, seeded from its round's training seed, from its training participants only. Their values are the stored zero-shot scores of those bags' segments, so no extra model run is needed.

## Training

Fixed on 2026-11-25, before any training run and before any test score is computed. The settings are in `configs/pilot/train.yaml`, which the arm files `configs/pilot/b0.yaml`, `b1.yaml` and `cpg.yaml` extend.

- **Arms and what trains.** LoRA adds two small trainable matrices beside a frozen weight matrix; the second starts at 0, so every run starts from the untrained model. It sits on the 7 projections (q, k, v, o, gate, up and down) of each of the language model's 30 layers, 210 places, picked by their full names so that the encoder's own q, k and v get none. The adapter between the encoder and the language model trains as a copy, saved with LoRA, at its own learning rate of 2.0e-5; the original stays frozen. The encoder never runs in training, which reads the pinned caches. B0 trains on CE(`x0`), the usual cross-entropy loss of a yes-or-no answer, with class weights 1, computed from z, the score fixed in section "Fixed settings": the model's raw score (logit) for "Yes" minus its raw score for "No". B1 adds 0.25 CE(`x1`) + 0.5 CE(`x2`), and CPG adds λG, the weight of the gain term, times that term. B1 is CPG's code with λG = 0: it computes the gain and its running statistics without training on them, logs them like CPG and starts them as CPG does (section "Encoder caches and zero-shot scores", bullet "Start of the running statistics"), so it also needs the zero-shot scores.
- **Bags and epochs.** A step is one update of the trained weights from 4 bags (2 positive, 2 negative); a step that the loss scaler skips (bullet "Number formats and repeatability") makes no update but still counts. An epoch is one pass of the bag sampler. In each epoch, a participant with n segments offers the 4·⌊min(n, 16)/4⌋ that were used least often so far (8 in place of 16 under the fallback of check F5), ties drawn at random, so that leftovers wait; they are shuffled and cut into bags of 4. Within each class, the bags are ranked by their participant's trained share (bags trained over bags offered in earlier epochs; 0 in epoch 1), lowest first, ties at random. An epoch ends when a class has fewer than 2 bags left; in every pilot fold that is the positive class, after about 84 steps in an average fold (82 to 92 by fold), the same in every epoch. With k steps in the epoch, the first 2k bags of each class train; they are shuffled within their class, and each step takes the next 2 of each. Participants whose bags were left over (about 56% of the negative bags each epoch) then have a lower share and are chosen earlier in the next epoch, while the shuffle keeps the order of the steps from following earlier use. A bag counts as trained, and its segments as used, once the sampler places it in a step of the epoch, so the sampler takes nothing from the training itself, and a skipped step changes no bag. Two bags of one participant may share a step.
- **Who trains, and seeds.** A fold-run's training participants are those of the round's four other folds, once the partitions match their pinned SHA-256; a mismatch stops the run. No part of a training fold is held out, and there is no early stopping and no tuning. The study design, which is not in this repository, kept 15% of each training fold as inner validation, for logs only. No check, rule or decision of the pilot reads it, and it would cost about 23 of a fold's about 151 training participants while check F3 needs B0 to learn, so it is dropped (2026-11-25). Round 1 uses seed 101 and round 2 seed 102, for the partitions and for training. Each fold-run has one random stream for its bags and one seed for PyTorch, set before LoRA is added, both derived from the round's seed and the fold and the same for every arm. B0, B1 and CPG of a fold-run therefore see the same bags in the same order and start from the same LoRA values, so the two sides of Δ, which is taken fold by fold, are paired. A 4th epoch, if one is added, keeps the bags of the first three.
- **Calls.** A bag goes through the model view by view: `x1` first, whose bag mean is kept without gradient for the gain, then `x0`, then `x2`. Every one-chunk clip gives the language model the same number of input positions, so a bag's one-chunk clips of one view share one call and need no padding, the empty positions that give the inputs of one call a common length; in tests on a small random model with Voxtral's layout, padding moved z slightly. The `x0` and `x2` clips of the one segment over 30 s, which have two chunks, get a call of their own. Apart from LoRA's dropout, which acts only in training, a training call thus computes what scoring computes, though four clips in one call may round slightly differently from one (the GPU tests print the difference). After all calls of a bag and view comes one backward pass, so the gain acts on the mean z of all 4 clips and each clip's CE counts one quarter. Every layer uses gradient checkpointing: it keeps only its input and is computed again in the backward pass, which saves memory at the cost of about one more forward pass, and it changed no bit on the small model.
- **The gain term.** For a bag with label y (1 if PHQ-8-positive, 0 if not), z̄0 and z̄1 are its mean z on `x0` and `x1`, and ẑ0 and ẑ1 are these means minus their view's running mean μ, divided by its running spread σ. The gain is G_b = (2y − 1)·ẑ0 − max(0, (2y − 1)·ẑ1). Its second part is the reference that `x0` must beat: how far the words-only view `x1` leans to the right label, which the clamp max(0, ·) sets to 0 when `x1` leans the wrong way. The gain term is the soft hinge s·log(1 + exp((m − G_b)/s)), with the margin m and the softness s of the fixed settings, and the hinge is active on a bag when G_b < m; ẑ1, μ and σ pass no gradient. The gain is computed in at least fp32: fp16 inputs are cast to fp32 before any arithmetic, and float64 inputs stay float64, so the code's tests can compare it with known values in float64. μ and σ come from running averages of the bag means and of their squares, with momentum 0.9: each update keeps 0.9 of the old average and adds 0.1 of the new value. They update once per step, after its 4 bags, from the mean of their bag means and the mean of their squares, for both views at the same point and also on a skipped step, whose forward values are finite; all 4 gains of a step use the values from before it. For each view, the two averages start at the mean and the mean square of the zero-shot bag means of the 32 start bags (section "Encoder caches and zero-shot scores", bullet "Start of the running statistics"). σ is the square root of the average square minus the squared average, taken as 0 if rounding makes that difference negative, because its root would be NaN, which no floor catches. σ then never falls below 0.1, nor below a quarter of the start spread: the population SD of the 32 start bag means, computed in the same way.
- **Loss and schedules.** Each CE is the mean over the bag's 4 clips, and a step's loss is the mean of its 4 bags' losses, so the balance between CE and the gain stays as written. A run's number of steps T follows from its fold-run's sampler before step 0, is the same for its three arms, and is recorded. The learning rate is 0 at step 0, rises in a straight line over ⌈0.05·T⌉ steps, then falls along half a cosine to 0, for LoRA and the adapter alike, each from its own rate; λG = min(1, t/⌈0.2·T⌉) at step t, counted from 0. In an average fold, T = 252 for 3 epochs, with 13 warm-up steps and a ramp of 51. A 4th epoch or the 8-segment fallback changes T, and both schedules follow it; both also move on with a skipped step. AdamW, the optimiser, keeps its defaults (betas 0.9 and 0.999, eps 1.0e-8, no AMSGrad), written into `configs/pilot/train.yaml`, and its weight decay applies to every trained tensor, none of which is a bias or a norm. The gradient clip, which shrinks all gradients together when their joint size is above 1.0, acts after the loss scale is taken out.
- **Number formats and repeatability.** Every trained number is kept in fp32, as master weights, while the forward pass computes in fp16 (autocast). PyTorch's dynamic loss scaler multiplies the loss before the backward pass, so that small fp16 gradients do not round to zero. With its defaults, written into `configs/pilot/train.yaml`, it starts at 2¹⁶ (65,536), halves the scale and skips the step when a gradient overflows, and doubles it after 2,000 clean steps; a skipped step leaves the weights and the optimiser's state unchanged. PyTorch's strict repeatable mode uses the repeatable version of every operation and stops at any that has none; its warn-only mode only warns where an operation is not repeatable. Training runs in strict mode if the timing run (bullet "Check F5") shows that strict mode runs without an error and that its median step, over the 2nd to 10th timed steps, takes at most 10% longer than in warn-only mode. Otherwise it runs in warn-only mode, and a run is close to repeatable, not bit-identical, because some GPU operations of the backward pass are then not repeatable. `configs/pilot/train.yaml` says `pending` until the commit that records the timing run sets the mode by this rule, and every training run except the timing run, whose result sets the mode, refuses `pending`. Scoring always switches back to the zero-shot job's numeric settings first.
- **Check F1.** F1 is read in this way, a change of its row dated 2026-11-25. A dynamic loss scaler meets gradients that are not finite by design and skips the steps that hold them, so such a gradient no longer fails a run; skipped steps are counted and reported. A run fails at the first loss (before scaling) or z that is not finite, and also if the scale falls below 1, where even unscaled gradients overflow; a run has far fewer than 2,000 steps, so the scale never grows back, and a 17th skipped step fails it. "All 30 runs" means the runs that the pilot keeps after any pre-set cut (bullet "Check F5" and section "If time runs short"). A failed run is fixed and goes again from the start, and it is never read as a result; if CPG cannot be made to finish, no rule is read and the report says so. The study design, which is not in this repository, also had a fallback for a CPG that stays unstable: finish B0 and B1 and report CPG as not yet working, Amber at best. It is dropped (2026-11-25), because it reads a failed run as a result, and the decision rule is never read before the five checks pass.
- **Overflow in training.** The overflow watch (section "Encoder caches and zero-shot scores", bullet "Overflow") also runs on every forward pass of training. It pauses during the backward pass, whose recomputation would otherwise count some parts twice, and is read once per step. A flag ends its run as failed at that step, before the update; a run that finishes without a flag never reached the fp16 limit in any watched part. Every later run then uses the pre-set fp32 fallback, dated with the watch's counts as its evidence; the flagged run goes again with it, and the runs that finished stand. Their records show that they ran without it, and the report names them, since the arms of one fold-run may then differ in that layer's format. After a flag in a training run, a fallback that cannot run stops the runs, and no new setting is made. A flag in the timing run comes before any test score: the fallback is dated in the same way, and the timing run goes again with it. If the fallback cannot run there, or makes check F5 fail by a projection above 4 GPU-hours, the researcher decides before any training run and before the zero-shot job (section "Encoder caches and zero-shot scores", bullet "Overflow"), in place of the 8-segment trigger of the bullet "Check F5". A run reads the cache checks of all four versions, `rev` included because the run scores it, and refuses to start while one of them has flagged the adapter or the language model and the fallback is not in place.
- **Logged measures.** Each step writes one line of a log in the workspace, with no participant ID: the loss and its parts, the loss scale and whether the step was skipped, μ and σ before the update, and counts over the step's bags, from which shares are formed per epoch. The counts are the bags and the active bags, by class and by whether the words were right, and how often σ sits at its floor. The words are right when (2y − 1)·ẑ1 > 0, the clamp's own test, so in a words-wrong bag the clamp sets the reference to 0. The pushes of CE and of the gain, the sizes of their gradients, are logged side by side: CE's as the mean |sigmoid(z) − y| over the step's `x0` clips, where sigmoid(z) is the probability of "Yes" against "No", and the gain's as the mean λG·sigmoid((m − G_b)/s)/σ0 over its bags, σ0 being the running spread of `x0`. |z0|/|z1|, the mean |z̄0| over the mean |z̄1| of the step's bags, shows whether `x0` grows more confident than `x1`. The offset part is the signed mean of the gain's gradient on z̄0 over the step's bags, logged beside its mean size: pushes of equal size on the two classes cancel in it, while an offset does not. Each epoch reports the measured mean and spread (population SD) of its bag means beside its average μ and σ, to show whether the running statistics follow them. B0 has no gain, so it logs the loss, the loss scale, whether the step was skipped and z, and none of the gain's measures.
- **F2 and the two allowed rules.** The gain-alive share of a CPG run is the number of bags with G_b < m, as computed in the loss, over all bags of epochs 2 and 3 together; each epoch's share, by class and by whether the words were right, is reported beside it. "Fewer than 20%" means a share below 0.20 and "more than 80%" one above 0.80, so a share of exactly 0.20 or 0.80 changes nothing. F2 passes if every CPG run's share lies between 0.20 and 0.80, both included; the pooled share over all kept CPG runs is reported. The 4-epoch rule reads "the training loss" as its CE part, CE(`x0`) + 0.25 CE(`x1`) + 0.5 CE(`x2`), a change dated 2026-11-25; the total and the gain term are reported beside it. The gain term moves with the running statistics and rises as `x1` learns and its reference grows, so the total can rise while every CE falls. The CE part is clearly falling if its mean over the second half of epoch 3's steps lies below its mean over the first half by more than 2 standard errors of the difference, from the per-step values, and by at least 5% of the first half's mean. With an odd number of steps, the halves are the first and the last half, rounded down, and the middle step is left out. As the chosen bags are shuffled within each epoch, the two halves hold, on average, bags of the same earlier use, so familiarity alone does not lower the second half.
- **Check F5.** F5 times the training part of a three-view run (B1 or CPG), from the start of the job to the saved weights; scoring is timed apart. Before any trained run, a timing run of CPG on round 1, fold 1, times the first 10 steps of the sampler in warn-only mode and the next 10 in strict mode, on one loaded model, an error counting as strict mode not running; the first timed step of each mode warms up and is left out. It projects that time as the load time (from the job's start until the model and its inputs are loaded) plus T for 3 epochs times the median seconds of the 2nd to 10th timed steps, in the mode that the timing sets (bullet "Number formats and repeatability"). It keeps no weights, writes no score and starts μ and σ from stand-in values, so it needs no zero-shot score. If the projection is above 4 GPU-hours, the 8-segment fallback applies before B0's round 1, dated with the timing as its evidence, and B1's round 2 is dropped with it; this trigger is added to F5's row on 2026-11-25. F5 itself is read on the training hours of the first CPG run of round 1, fold 1, that finishes (at 8 segments if the projection set them), times 4/3 if the 4-epoch rule adds an epoch, and is recorded in the same dated commit as the two allowed rules. If it fails at 16 segments, every arm uses 8, B0's round 1 and that CPG run go again with 8, the readings of m and of the epochs stand, since each is made once, and B1's round 2 is dropped at once. If F5 still fails at 8 segments, no pre-set fallback is left: F5 fails as a check, so the decision rule is not read (section "Feasibility checks"), and the researcher decides, before the remaining runs, which of them still run, within the cut order of section "If time runs short" and with no new setting.
- **Scoring.** Each run scores its own test fold in the same job, right after its weights are saved. The training model, its optimiser and its gradients are first released and the GPU is checked to hold nothing of the training model, because the two models would not fit on it together. The saved file is then loaded into a fresh model built without the encoder, and LoRA and the adapter are cast to fp16, so what is scored is the kept file, in the format that training computes in. Every segment of the test fold is scored on `x0` and `rev`, from the cache, one clip per call, by the zero-shot job's own scoring code with the watch on. A flag or a z that is not finite writes no scores: the run then fails and goes again from the start, and a flag is met as in training (bullet "Overflow in training"). The scoring receives no labels, and of the test-fold scores the job prints and records only numbers that need none (counts, whether all are finite, means, spreads, percentiles and the share above 0), as the zero-shot job does. A run writes one z per segment and view; a participant's mean, the AUROCs, Δ and check F3 come later from the analysis code. The weights, the log and the scores stay in the workspace on OMNI, and only aggregates are printed or committed.
- **Order, release tag and fallbacks.** The tests of the code on the CPU and on a GPU and the timing run come first; if a bag's call does not fit on the GPU, the researcher decides before any test score exists. Next comes the commit that records their outcomes (the repeatability mode, F5's projection and any fallback), and only then the zero-shot job, which also refuses to start while `configs/pilot/train.yaml` says `pending`. So every outcome of these first jobs is settled before any test score exists, by the researcher (memory, an overflow fallback that cannot run or makes F5 fail) or by the rules above (the repeatability mode, F5's projection). B0's round 1 needs no zero-shot score and may run beside the zero-shot job; the first CPG run of round 1, fold 1, waits until the zero-shot scores are pinned, and its training logs decide the two allowed rules and F5. The settings then freeze and are released as version 0.1.0 of the code (tag `v0.1.0`), and the other runs follow. Until a `v0.1.x` tag exists, the training job refuses every run but the timing run, B0's round 1 and that first CPG run; once one exists, every run, reruns of these included, must come from a checkout at the newest `v0.1.x` tag, so that a fallback released after the freeze applies to every later run. Once test scores exist, only the two allowed changes and the pre-set fallbacks (section "Allowed setting changes") can change a setting; the fallbacks of F5 (8 segments) and of the overflow watch (the flagged layers in fp32) may be used then, since both were fixed before any score. Each fallback used is dated in the settings change log below, with its evidence, and in the repository's CHANGELOG, in one commit, which after the freeze is released as the next `v0.1.x`.

## Allowed setting changes

Besides the pre-set fallbacks in the feasibility checks, two changes are allowed. Each comes from training logs only and is made once, before the remaining runs start. It applies to all arms, is dated in the settings change log below and is sent to the supervisor in one line.

- **Gain-alive rule.** One CPG run (round 1, fold 1) runs before any other CPG run. If the hinge is active on fewer than 20% of bags in epochs 2 and 3, m becomes 0.5. If it is active on more than 80%, m becomes 0.1.
- **4-epoch rule.** If the training loss of that same CPG run (its CE part since 2026-11-25, section "Training") is still clearly falling in epoch 3, every arm trains 4 epochs, and B0's round 1 is rerun with 4 epochs.

If m or the number of epochs changes, that first CPG run is redone with the new setting.

## If time runs short

Cut in this order: B1's round 2, then B1, then round 2 of B0 and CPG. The minimum version (B0 and CPG, round 1 only, both test views) is read with the same rule and a wider error. The rule is never read before the five checks pass.
